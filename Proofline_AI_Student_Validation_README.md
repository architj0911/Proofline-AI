# Proofline AI — Student Validation

## Overview

Proofline AI is designed to help students prepare to explain and defend technical claims on their resumes. It aims to ask adaptive questions about projects, identify knowledge gaps, and help students prepare supporting evidence.

To investigate this problem, an initial Google Forms survey collected **8 responses on 10 October 2026**. All respondents identified themselves as **third-year students**.

> **Important limitation:** This is a small, preliminary convenience sample, not a representative study of all students or proof of broad market demand. Findings below describe only these eight responses. One respondent skipped the tool-interest question, and five skipped the final open-ended question.

## Respondent profile and project experience

| Survey result | Responses | Share of all 8 respondents |
|---|---:|---:|
| Third-year students | 8/8 | 100% |
| Have worked on multiple technical projects | 5/8 | 62.5% |
| Have worked on one technical project | 1/8 | 12.5% |
| Have not yet worked on projects but plan to | 2/8 | 25% |

Six respondents (75%) reported having worked on one or more technical projects. Two (25%) planned to work on projects but had not yet done so.

## Explaining technical projects

When asked how often they had struggled to explain the technical details of a resume project:

| Response | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Frequently | 3 | 37.5% |
| Sometimes | 3 | 37.5% |
| Rarely | 2 | 25% |

Six of eight respondents (75%) said they **frequently or sometimes** struggled to explain technical details. This suggests that explaining project work is a relevant preparation challenge in this small sample.

## Interview experience

| Response to being asked about a technology or project they could not confidently explain | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Yes, several times | 3 | 37.5% |
| Yes, once | 2 | 25% |
| Not yet | 1 | 12.5% |
| Have not attended technical interviews | 2 | 25% |

Five respondents (62.5%) said they had encountered this situation at least once. Two had not attended technical interviews, so their answers do not establish whether they would encounter this issue in an interview.

## Hardest parts of preparing to defend projects

This was a multiple-selection question. The counts below indicate how many respondents selected each option; percentages use all 8 respondents as the denominator.

| Challenge selected | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Identifying gaps in their knowledge | 5 | 62.5% |
| Answering follow-up technical questions | 3 | 37.5% |
| Proving what they personally built | 2 | 25% |
| Explaining architecture and design decisions | 1 | 12.5% |
| Remembering implementation details | 1 | 12.5% |
| Reported not having faced this problem | 1 | 12.5% |
| Other | 1 | 12.5% |

The most frequently selected challenge was **identifying gaps in knowledge** (5 of 8). Follow-up questions were selected by 3 respondents, while 2 selected proving what they personally built. These findings align with Proofline AI's proposed focus on adaptive questioning, explicit knowledge gaps, and distinguishing a resume statement from supporting evidence.

## Current preparation methods

This was a multiple-selection question.

| Method selected | Count | Share of all 8 respondents |
|---|---:|---:|
| YouTube tutorials | 7 | 87.5% |
| AI tools | 3 | 37.5% |
| Documentation and notes | 2 | 25% |
| Friends or mentors | 2 | 25% |
| No specific method | 1 | 12.5% |

YouTube tutorials were the most commonly selected preparation method. Respondents could select more than one method, so these counts do not sum to eight.

## Interest in the proposed tool

One respondent skipped this question. Percentages in the following table use **7 answers** as the denominator.

| Response | Count | Share of 7 answers |
|---|---:|---:|
| Definitely | 2 | 28.6% |
| Probably | 3 | 42.9% |
| Not sure | 1 | 14.3% |
| Probably not | 1 | 14.3% |
| No answer | 1 | Not included in denominator |

Five of the seven respondents who answered (71.4%) said they would **definitely or probably** use such a tool. This is an encouraging early signal of interest, but it is not proof of actual adoption or willingness to pay. The survey did not measure usage after trying a prototype.

## Open-ended feedback

Three respondents provided a nonblank answer to the final open-ended question:

- One respondent said the tool would help identify knowledge gaps.
- One emphasized accurate and personalized results, ease of use, and time savings. They raised concerns about privacy, data security, incorrect information, reliability, and trust.
- One answered “Nothing,” which does not provide a specific feature request or concern.

Five respondents left this question blank. These responses are reported as received; no additional feedback has been inferred.

## What these findings mean for Proofline AI

Based on this initial sample, the most defensible product priorities are:

1. **Identify knowledge gaps:** Make missing concepts and areas to review clear after each answer.
2. **Ask adaptive follow-up questions:** Probe the specific project and the student's previous answer rather than relying only on generic interview questions.
3. **Connect claims to evidence:** Help users distinguish what their resume states from what supporting text or project evidence is available.
4. **Make evaluations trustworthy:** Explain scores and feedback, and avoid presenting uncertain AI judgements as facts.
5. **Protect privacy:** Explain how resume data is processed, avoid unnecessary retention, and keep provider credentials server-side.

These are product hypotheses informed by the survey, not claims that respondents tested or endorsed each implemented feature.

## Limitations

- The sample contains only 8 respondents, all of whom reported being in their third year.
- The survey is not shown to be a random or representative sample.
- One respondent skipped the tool-interest question; five skipped the final open-ended question.
- Stated willingness to use a tool is not the same as actual usage, retention, or willingness to pay.
- The survey captures self-reported experiences and does not independently verify interview outcomes.
- The results should not be generalized to all students without additional research.

## Conclusion

In this initial survey, **6 of 8 respondents (75%) reported frequently or sometimes struggling to explain technical project details**, and **5 of 7 respondents who answered (71.4%) said they would definitely or probably use the proposed tool**. Identifying knowledge gaps was the most frequently selected preparation challenge.

These results provide preliminary support for investigating resume-driven technical defense practice. The next step is to test the working Proofline AI prototype with students, gather feedback on the quality and usefulness of its questions and evaluations, and document what changed as a result. The survey results alone should not be presented as proof of broad market demand.

## Method

- **Instrument:** Google Forms survey about technical projects, interview preparation, and interest in an adaptive resume-defense tool.
- **Responses analysed:** 8.
- **Collection date:** 10 October 2026, based on the timestamps in the exported response file.
- **Privacy:** This summary omits respondent names and email addresses; the exported response file supplied for analysis contained response timestamps and answers but no name or email columns.
